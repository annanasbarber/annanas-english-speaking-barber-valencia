Why does communication matter when choosing a barber?

A haircut begins with a conversation. Before picking up the scissors or clippers, a barber should understand what the client wants to achieve. That means discussing the desired length, shape and hairstyle, as well as any concerns about the current haircut.

This approach is consistent with the UK's National Occupational Standards for barbering, which include consultation, hair analysis and agreeing on the intended result before cutting.

At ANNANAS, we believe the most important part of the conversation is finding a common understanding between the barber and the client.

What if your hair is too short for the style in your reference photo?

A photograph is a useful starting point, but the barber still needs to assess whether the desired hairstyle is achievable with the client's current hair length and characteristics.

We experienced this with another English-speaking client who visited ANNANAS carrying a photograph of a haircut he liked. His hair was shorter than the length required to achieve that particular result.

After listening to his preferences, our barber suggested an alternative suited to his existing hair length. He also recommended a styling product from American Crew to help the client manage his hairstyle at home.

The National Occupational Standards for creative haircutting likewise include assessing hair length, discussing achievable looks and using visual references during consultation.

How can you tell if a barbershop in Valencia is right for you?

  1. Check genuine reviews and photographs. Read experiences from real customers, especially reviews with photographs of haircut results.
  2. Look for clear information in English. The website should make services, prices and booking options easy to understand.
  3. Pay attention to the consultation. A professional barber should listen, ask follow-up questions, assess your hair and propose alternatives when necessary. Both of you should understand the intended result before the haircut begins.

The WorldSkills Occupational Standards for Hairdressing also emphasise client communication, consultation and agreement on the service.

Our advice: Speaking English helps, but the most important thing is making sure the barber understands what you want and that you both agree on the result.
